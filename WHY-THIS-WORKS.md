# What is this thing, actually?

Imagine you're building the world's biggest, coolest LEGO castle. Every day you add a
few new pieces. But what if, every morning, someone snuck in and knocked your castle
back down to a pile of loose bricks? You'd have to start over. Every. Single. Day.

That's what research feels like *without* a system like this. You read an article about
a company, you have a great call with a founder, you think of a smart idea in the
shower — and then it just... evaporates. A week later you're re-Googling the same
company because you forgot what you already figured out.

**This tool is the opposite of that.** It's a notebook that never forgets, that an AI
helps you write in, and that gets *smarter to use* the more you use it — not because the
notebook is magic, but because of a few boring, deliberate choices about how it's built.

## Why "just files on your computer" is actually the clever part

This isn't an app. It's not a website. It's not a database somewhere in the cloud that
you're renting access to. It's just plain text files, sitting on your own laptop, in a
folder, like a Word document or a photo.

Why does that matter?

- **You truly own it.** Nobody can shut it down, change the price, or lock you out. It's
  yours the same way a notebook in your desk drawer is yours.
- **You can open it with anything.** Notepad, a text editor, Obsidian, `grep` in a
  terminal, or an AI assistant — it's all just text, so nothing can ever trap your notes
  inside one company's app.
- **It's easy to trust.** If something looks wrong, you can just... look at the file.
  There's no hidden database query you can't see. What you see is what's actually there.

## Why the notes link to each other (the Obsidian part)

Each company, market, or idea gets its own file. But the real trick is that files link
to *each other* — like "this heat pump company" links to "the HVAC market" links to
"that idea I had about installer costs." Over time this turns into a web, not a pile.

Think of it like a spiderweb instead of a stack of index cards. A stack of cards only
helps if you remember which card to pull out. A web means you can start *anywhere* and
follow the threads to everything connected to it — even things you forgot were related.

## Why it compounds — the actual magic

Here's the part that matters most: **every note you save today makes tomorrow's
research faster**, because your AI assistant reads the whole web before it answers you.

Ask about a company today — it checks if you've researched something like it before.
Have a call next month with a competitor — it already remembers what the first company
told you, so it knows what sharper questions to ask. Six months from now, you're not
starting from zero on *anything* you've already touched. You're always building on top
of what's already there.

It's like a snowball rolling downhill: small at first, but every rotation picks up more
snow, and it picks up snow *faster* the bigger it gets. A notebook that starts empty is
just a notebook. A notebook that's been fed for six months is a research partner that
knows your whole world — and it only gets more valuable from here.
